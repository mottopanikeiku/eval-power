I measure historical user preferences in the public Arena Human Preference 55k release, not a current leaderboard. I download only the model and winner columns from its pinned Parquet conversion. I retain unordered model-pair counts for A wins, B wins, and ties. The source pools ordinary ties and both-bad ties, so I cannot distinguish them. Model names remain; user text, user IDs, and source row IDs do not.

I split individual votes at random into disjoint pilot and heldout counts before fitting. Binomial allocation within each pair/outcome cell is exactly equivalent to independently allocating its individual votes. Exposure-based eligibility uses the total sample, not estimated scores. The default cutoff is 500 votes involving a model; votes with an ineligible opponent are then removed. All retained zero-count pair cells remain in the matrices.

The votes.npz model array is alphabetical. Count rows follow numpy.triu_indices(number_of_models, 1); outcome columns are A win, B win, tie. Its pilot and heldout arrays sum to the source count matrix. The bootstrap.npz score columns are alphabetical exposure-eligible models, equivalently alphabetical model names in ranking.csv. Each bootstrap row is one successful fit; summary.json records all attempted fits and failure counts.

I fit an unpenalized Bradley-Terry likelihood. For a model pair, the win probability is logistic(score_A - score_B). A tie contributes half the log probability of each outcome, as one observation. I center scores to sum zero. I multiply log-odds scores by 400/log(10) to display conventional Elo-scale differences. A disconnected comparison graph has no global score differences; a directed win graph that is not strongly connected has no finite maximum-likelihood estimate. I report these failures rather than use a prior or ridge penalty.

I compare pointwise inverse-Hessian 95% normal intervals with pointwise percentile intervals from an IID vote bootstrap. A multinomial draw over pair/outcome cells exactly reproduces resampling individual votes with replacement. The Hessian treats fractional ties as curvature observations; it is not a sandwich covariance estimator and need not match the empirical vote variability. Bootstrap intervals are conditional on successful fits, and every disconnected, separated, or numerical failure is counted separately. These intervals do not have simultaneous coverage and do not correct for selecting neighboring models by the same data used to fit their ranks.

For the descriptive rank neighbors I calculate the total number of Arena votes needed for a two-sided alpha=0.05 normal contrast test to reach 80% power if the fitted gap and observed matchup mix remained fixed. The calculation scales the contrast variance inversely with total vote count. This is a total network-vote count, not a number of direct comparisons between the two models. A zero pilot gap has no finite plan; an unidentified gap has no plan. Neither is dropped silently.

For a separate planning check I choose evenly spaced alphabetical-neighbor pairs, independent of estimated ranks or gaps. I estimate gaps and standard errors from pilot votes only and resample the disjoint heldout pair/outcome counts at the planned total vote count. I refit every resample and report the two-sided Wald rejection rate. The all-trial rate counts failed fits as nonrejections; a successful-fit-conditional rate is also saved. Sampling with replacement can request more votes than the heldout sample contains. It reuses historical votes and is not collection of new votes or a prospective estimate of power. Monte Carlo standard errors describe resampling uncertainty only, not uncertainty about the heldout population.

The rejection check is two-sided. A significant difference opposite to the pilot direction still counts as a rejection, so the rate is not the probability of confirming the pilot's preferred model. In pilot_planning.csv, RWKV-4-Raven-14B versus alpaca-13b has opposite pilot and heldout gap signs. The llama-2-7b-chat versus llama2-70b-steerlm-chat plan has a heldout resampling rejection rate of 0.09 despite the 0.80 planning target.

I assume independent votes and one transitive preference strength per model. Repeated users, repeated prompts, changing prompts, matchup assignment, and model changes can violate these assumptions. The compact aggregate has no clustering labels. This historical competition subset is not representative of all Arena traffic. I make no claim about local execution speed or future leaderboard stability.
